Services content concept

Compliance and site services, explained clearly.

CTS helps clients inspect, test, document, maintain, and repair the systems that keep their sites safe, compliant, and operational.

Routine compliance

Monthly Inspections

Recurring site reviews that help identify issues early, document conditions, and keep ongoing responsibilities organized.

What is it?

A scheduled review of applicable equipment, records, alarms, and visible site conditions.

Why is it important?

Regular inspections can uncover developing problems before they cause a failed inspection, compliance gap, or disruption.

Who needs it?

Operators whose equipment, permits, or regulatory programs require recurring inspection and documentation.

How CTS helps

CTS coordinates visits, documents findings, communicates next steps, and helps clients follow through on identified issues.

Regulatory readiness

Compliance Testing

Testing that helps verify applicable systems meet federal, state, local, permit, and equipment requirements.

What is it?

Required or recommended testing performed on a schedule determined by the site, system, jurisdiction, and due date.

Why is it important?

Testing can confirm system performance, surface deficiencies, and help prevent penalties or unexpected failures.

Who needs it?

Owners and operators responsible for regulated storage, monitoring, containment, or related site systems.

How CTS helps

CTS identifies the applicable scope, coordinates testing, explains results, and helps plan the next action when a deficiency is found.

Documentation support

Third-Party Reporting

Preparation and submission support for reports that must be provided to regulators, clients, property stakeholders, or other third parties.

What is it?

Organizing service results and site information into the required report or documentation package.

Why is it important?

Complete, timely reporting creates a clear record of compliance activity and helps prevent administrative gaps.

Who needs it?

Clients with reporting obligations tied to inspections, testing, permits, contracts, or regulatory programs.

How CTS helps

CTS prepares documentation, coordinates supporting records, and submits reports on a client’s behalf where the applicable process allows.

System integrity

Hydrostatic Testing

Integrity testing for applicable containment systems and components using the method approved for the equipment and jurisdiction.

What is it?

A controlled test used to assess whether an applicable system or component maintains integrity under defined conditions.

Why is it important?

Integrity testing can identify leaks or weaknesses before they lead to environmental risk, downtime, or a compliance failure.

Who needs it?

Sites whose equipment, permit, repair history, or regulatory program requires an approved integrity test.

How CTS helps

CTS confirms the applicable scope, coordinates testing, documents results, and explains any follow-up work indicated by the test.

Operational response

Service & Repair

Corrective support for equipment problems and deficiencies discovered during inspections, testing, alarms, or daily operation.

What is it?

Diagnosis, adjustment, repair, or replacement work intended to restore the applicable equipment or system.

Why is it important?

Timely repairs can limit downtime, restore compliance, and prevent a smaller equipment issue from becoming more disruptive.

Who needs it?

Any site with a documented deficiency, failed component, alarm condition, or operational concern within CTS’s service capabilities.

How CTS helps

CTS evaluates the issue, recommends a practical path forward, completes or coordinates the repair, and documents the outcome.

Team readiness

Operator Training

Practical instruction that helps site teams understand equipment, safety procedures, regulatory responsibilities, and the right response to common conditions.

What is it?

Role- and site-aware guidance covering operation, routine responsibilities, warning conditions, records, and escalation steps.

Why is it important?

Prepared operators are better able to prevent mistakes, respond to alarms, maintain records, and recognize when expert help is needed.

Who needs it?

Owners, managers, and employees whose roles include operating or overseeing regulated site equipment and compliance tasks.

How CTS helps

CTS connects regulatory expectations to the client’s actual site and gives operators practical guidance they can use day to day.
